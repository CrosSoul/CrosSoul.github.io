The case study focuses on three aspects:

  1. How players learn attraction and routing rules
  2. How electric arcs affect both power and traversal
  3. How a puzzle tool becomes protection and a means to counterattack

01 / RULES

Attracting lightning and routing power

Natural lightning targets

Natural lightning first checks sky access, then tip material priority. When those conditions are equal, the higher rod wins. Rail controls, tip swaps, and placement let players change the target.

Power routing and passage states

An energized rod can activate a receiver directly or feed a chain of connectors. Each connector rotates in 90-degree increments. Green receivers stay active after one charge; blue receivers require continued power.

Portable rod constraints

The player can carry one mechanism part at a time. A portable rod only works in a socket: carrying it or leaving it on the ground does not provide protection.

02 / TEACHING

From predicting a target to changing its conditions

Introduce one variable before adding priority and new interactions, then combine familiar conditions in later areas.

01

Height: establish a readable relationship

The tutorial starts with comparable rods and a button-operated rail. With material and sky access held equal, the moving rod must rise above its competitor to change the strike target. Slot lights show the rail’s current step.

This first step connects the player’s action, a visible height change, and the strike result.

02

Material: revise the height-only expectation

The next setup uses different material priorities, allowing a lower rod with a stronger tip to receive the strike. The player must extend the height rule by recognizing material priority.

03

Tip swapping: apply the observed rule

After material priority is established, the player can remove, install, and swap tips. They must place the stronger tip on the rod connected to the required mechanism.

The Tower then adds connector rotation, carrying, and blocked passages. The Ruin adds sky obstruction and portable rods, expanding combinations without making every interaction available at once.

03 / SPATIAL PUZZLES

Electrical routes also change the player’s route

The Tower places circuits in traversable space: power must reach its target while the player retains a route to the exit or reward.

Blocked arcs and alternate routes

In the first blockage-focused puzzle, energizing a mechanism unlocks a chest behind a wall, but the resulting arc blocks access. The solution uses two mechanism-controlled doors to create an alternate route.

Successful wiring and access to the destination must be considered together.

Constrain the operating position

An arc across a path is insufficient if the player can operate the connector from the other side. Walls and platform edges constrain where the player can stand, making the effect on their route part of the puzzle.

A 45-degree starting orientation and two circuit stages

Connectors still rotate in 90-degree steps. Some begin at a 45-degree offset, with sockets positioned for diagonal connections, allowing a new circuit arrangement through the same interaction.

First, the blue route opens the ground-floor door. The player then changes sockets and orientations to build the magenta route through that door and reach the next connector. The same space must be reconfigured in two stages.

Additional circuit puzzle: crossing a maze with limited connectors

This puzzle began as a paper route, then used nine square structures to divide a grid. Sockets mark nodes, obstacles restrict routes, and a limited connector supply constrains the solution. The overhead image explains the route; play requires locating those connections from a third-person view.

04 / COMBAT APPLICATION

Using the rod for protection and counterattacks

The component stays familiar, but the task changes from satisfying natural-lightning conditions to establishing a protected position before an attack.

Enemy lightning changes the purpose of positioning

Natural strikes compare height, material, and sky access. Enemy special attacks charge before targeting the player. Standing within range of an installed rod redirects the attack; outside that range, the player is still hit.

A shrinking ring and text communicate the timing, prompting the player to use a familiar puzzle component for protection.

Carry the rod between attacks

After the third seal is removed, the Boss can attack the player along the approach. Sockets are spaced along the route. The player carries one portable rod and moves during the roughly ten-second cooldown and subsequent charge-up.

Picking up the rod removes protection, and one installed position cannot cover the entire route. Before the strike, the player must stop and install the rod at the next socket, then continue after the attack.

Charge cannons and reposition during cooldown

Three cannons have corresponding rod sockets. Redirected lightning charges the associated cannon, which fires automatically after repeated charges and damages the Boss.

After firing, the cannon enters cooldown and cannot charge. The player moves the rod to another available cannon. Green, yellow, and red lights indicate availability, charging, and cooldown.

The demo Boss uses only the special lightning attack and is defeated through cannon fire, keeping this encounter focused on the puzzle component’s combat application.

05 / ARCHITECTURE & PROGRESSION

An open-air lightning source serving indoor mechanisms

A central tower powers surrounding buildings

Rods need sky access, while the temple’s indoor mechanisms need power. This led to a central open courtyard and surrounding buildings: the tower attracts lightning and distributes it to indoor puzzles. Its dedicated tips also provide a recurring interaction point between stages.

Low, heavy Egyptian temple forms keep the lightning structures visually distinct and support the setting of stone ruins in a region of constant storms.

Three seals and an optional second half of the Tower

The main route moves from the central plaza through the tutorial courtyard, Tower, Ruin, and Boss approach. Each of the first three areas removes a seal. Since the Tower contains more puzzles, its seal is awarded around the halfway point, allowing players to continue the main route or explore the remaining puzzles.

Environment proposal: worship, research, and everyday life in the courtyard

The courtyard proposal uses altar motifs, equipment maintenance, gardens, and domestic objects to show how its inhabitants used lightning. Rods serve as religious symbols and protect living areas, while diagrams in the research area could hint at later mechanisms. This is an extension proposal, not a fully implemented environment.

06 / DESIGN DECISIONS

Changes and scope decisions during production

Height competition needs a local scope

With one shared source, each new rod competed with every earlier target, pushing later puzzles toward ever-higher positions. Strike Zones limit competition to each puzzle, and completed puzzle states are locked to avoid interference.

Find more puzzle variety in circuits and space

Height alone offered limited variety and constrained top-down observation and lower placements. I retained it as a teaching rule and developed more of the Tower around blocked passages, socket positions, and circuit reconfiguration.

Concentrate combat around the approach and cannons

I considered mobile enemies as puzzle power sources, but positioning, target selection, and behavior control added scope. The remote approach and cannon encounter reuse one special-strike system and concentrate combat on the rod mechanic.

Improve feedback for distant mechanisms

Doors opened from the control console are outside the player’s view, and large rods require repeated upward camera movement. Short camera shots could communicate those changes more clearly.

Contribution and asset sources

I designed the layouts and puzzles and implemented the Blueprint mechanisms, using AI for learning and debugging support. Health, flasks, HUD, bonfire interactions, and other foundations were adapted from my earlier demo.

Third-party assets include Epic’s Third Person template, Blocking Starter Pack, Free Spline VFX, Dragon for Boss Monster, Item Pickup VFX, KEStatues Lite, Rock Environment Pack, and Light Foliage. Arc endpoints, blocking behavior, and mechanism interactions were adapted for the project.
